## What is compromised running?

Compromised running is running while your legs and your cardiorespiratory system are already fatigued from other work. In a HYROX race that is the normal situation: after the SkiErg, the sleds, the burpee broad jumps and the other stations, you run 1 km again and again. HYROX itself recommends imitating that feeling in training, at home or in the gym.

## What does the research say and not say?

The research shows that running is the biggest part of the race. It does not show that running under fatigue is the most important factor. Brandt and colleagues (2025) had 11 recreational athletes run a simulated race.

| Finding | What the study found |
|---|---|
| Share of running | More than 60% of the time; a median of 51.2 minutes of running at a finish time of 86.5 minutes |
| Strongest relation to finish time | VO₂max, then training volume and body fat percentage |
| No clear relation | Grip strength and muscle mass |
| Highest lactate and RPE | On the stations, peaking at the wall balls |
| Running under fatigue | Called important, but not measured |

So training compromised running is a Benchmrk choice based on this research, not a proven main factor.

There is a hint from race results, though. In an analysis of more than 14,000 results in Open men, runs 5 to 8 were most strongly related to finish time. Those are the runs after the heaviest stations. Correlation is not causation, but it fits the picture that the second half of the race counts.

## How do you train compromised running?

You combine station work and running with no break in between. There are two ways to do that.

1. **In a sim.** You run part of the race: sections of running and stations in turn. How to build one is covered in [build a HYROX simulation](/en/guide/build-a-hyrox-simulation).
2. **With a compromised finisher.** After your threshold run you do a short block of station plus running. Handy if you can't do a sim this week.

You set the pace of your runs with your threshold pace as the base, see [run pacing](/en/guide/hyrox-run-pacing).

## What does an example workout look like?

Below is an example from the compromised running phase in the Benchmrk plan. T is your threshold pace.

| Workout | Content |
|---|---|
| Sim, compromised running phase | 8 × (750 m at race pace + station at 75% of race volume) |
| Goal of the sim | Run 8 within 12 s/km of run 1 |
| Compromised finisher | After the threshold run: 3 × (2 min wall balls, lunges or burpees, straight into 1 km at race pace) |
| Pace decay test (benchmark 4) | 8 × 1000 m with stations, log your splits |

Start easy. The goal is to run evenly, not to win the first kilometre. HYROX itself warns against starting too fast.

## Which stations make your legs the heaviest?

In the study, lactate and RPE were highest at the wall balls, the last station. Notably, the heaviest stations went the fastest. The sled push took 128 seconds on average and the sled pull 155 seconds, and heart rate and RPE dropped there. Specific tips are in [faster sled push](/en/guide/faster-sled-push) and [wall balls without stopping](/en/guide/wall-balls-without-stopping).

## How Benchmrk does it

In the compromised running phase, your weekly sim has sections of 750 m at race pace with station work in between. If you have no sim day, your threshold run gets a compromised finisher. Benchmarks 3 and 4 measure your splits and the decay between run 1 and run 8. The app uses that result for your prediction. How this fits in the whole plan, see [HYROX training plan](/en/hyrox-training-plan).
