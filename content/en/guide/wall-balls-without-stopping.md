## What are the rules of the wall balls?

You do 100 valid reps. According to the season 26/27 rulebook, every rep starts standing, with the ball in both hands. You squat until your hip is lower than your knee and throw the ball at the target with both hands.

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Depth | Hip below the knee |
| Target | Women 2.70 m, men 3.00 m |
| After the throw | Catch and carry on, or let the ball drop and start again standing |
| After a bounce | Do not catch it and carry straight on |
| Chalk | Not allowed; 2-minute penalty |
| Shoes | You may take them off at your target |

| Division | Weight of the ball |
|---|---|
| Women Open | 4 kg |
| Men Open and Women Pro | 6 kg |
| Men Pro | 9 kg |

All weights per division are in [Open or Pro](/en/guide/hyrox-open-vs-pro). A rep that does not meet the rules is a no-rep. It does not count, so it costs you time.

## Why are the wall balls so hard?

Because they are the last station, and you start them with a high heart rate. In the study by Brandt and colleagues (2025), heart rate, lactate and perceived effort were highest at the wall balls of the whole race. Maximum heart rate there was a median of 183 beats per minute, lactate 8.5 mmol/l and perceived effort 18 on the Borg scale of 6 to 20.

It is also the station where the differences are biggest. In a data analysis of over 14,000 results in Open men, from 15 races in 2024 and 2025, the wall balls had the biggest spread: a standard deviation of 2.02 minutes. Wall balls were also strongly linked to finish time. Rappelt and colleagues (2026) saw in seven seasons of Pro results that the wall balls were among the stations where placing shifted most relative to the final ranking.

## How do you divide the 100 reps?

In fixed sets with short breaks. No study has tested the best set size for HYROX. Pick a set size you can repeat when fatigued and hold it until the end.

| Approach | How |
|---|---|
| Fixed sets | For example 10 × 10, with a short, planned break |
| Break | Hold the ball or let it drop, a few breaths, go |
| Counting | Count your sets, not your reps |
| Avoid no-reps | Better a bit slower with full depth than redoing a rep |

In Doubles, Benchmrk splits the wall balls into sets of 10, taking turns. More on tactics in Doubles at [training for HYROX Doubles](/en/train-for-hyrox-doubles).

## How do you train the wall balls?

Mostly fatigued. Throwing 100 wall balls fresh says little about how it goes after 7 kilometres and 7 stations. In the Benchmrk strength plan, this station has an EMOM: every minute 12 to 15 wall balls at race weight, for 10 minutes, plus 3 × 5 front squats.

| Training | Content |
|---|---|
| Strength | EMOM 10 min: 12–15 wall balls at race weight + 3 × 5 front squats |
| Sim, compromised phase | 750 m at race pace, straight followed by 75 wall balls |
| Compromised finisher | 3 × (2 min wall balls, straight into 1 km at race pace) |
| Dress rehearsal | 100 wall balls as the last station, at race weight |

How to build the sim is in [building a HYROX simulation](/en/guide/build-a-hyrox-simulation). The running before it is run 8, see [run pacing](/en/guide/hyrox-run-pacing).

## What if you do not have a wall ball and target?

Use thrusters with a dumbbell or kettlebell, or squat-to-press with a backpack or sandbag. You miss the throwing and catching, but the movement and the fatigue are similar. More substitutes on [training for HYROX without a HYROX gym](/en/train-for-hyrox-without-a-hyrox-gym).

## How Benchmrk does it

If you say in the intake that wall balls are a weak station, you get a weekly EMOM at race weight with front squats. In the compromised phase, wall balls are in your sim or in the finisher after your threshold run. Your prediction shows how much time you lose at this station.
